    Regarding the blowing of captured rebel sepoys from guns, it's important to place this in context. The officers who ordered it and the soldiers who carried it out were themselves the victims of appalling cruelty which the mutineers/rebels had carried out on them and their families.

    For example, Nana Sahib and other leading rebels, on the approach of British forces, ordered that their hostages (British women and their children) must be killed. Two Muslim butchers, two Hindu peasants and one of Nana's bodyguards went into The Bibigarh. Armed with knives and hatchets they murdered the women and children. After the massacre the walls were covered in bloody hand prints, and the floor littered with fragments of human limbs. The dead and the dying were thrown down a nearby well; when the well was full, the 50-foot deep well was filled with remains to within 6 feet of the top, the remainder were thrown into the Ganges.

    This was only one of hundreds of atrocities committed against British families, so whilst deploring the actions of the army, one must recognise the background.

    There are two sides to every story.
